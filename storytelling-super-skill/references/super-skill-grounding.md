## Pyramid Of The Book

`Top claim`

- In the AI era, storytelling is the most durable human superpower because it drives attention, trust, memory, coordination, and action.

`Why this is true`

1. Story has a biological advantage.
2. We now compete inside a story economy.
3. AI makes average content cheap, so taste and authenticity become more valuable.
4. The capability can be trained through `Unlock -> Unleash -> Upgrade`.

`What this means in practice`

- Do not treat storytelling as decoration.
- Treat narrative as strategic infrastructure for leadership, persuasion, and positioning.
- Use AI as amplifier, not authorial replacement.

## Biological Advantage

- Treat story as a coordination technology, not merely a literary form.
- The user's supplied summary emphasizes three effects:
  - `neural coupling`: strong stories pull listeners into a shared mental track
  - `trust chemistry`: conflict and tension can intensify empathy and trust
  - `memory lift`: information inside stories is easier to retain than bare facts
- When writing from this lens, prioritize:
  - scene over abstraction
  - tension over flat explanation
  - felt consequence over generic claim
- If the user needs scientific certainty or publication-grade sourcing, verify any exact neuroscience or memory multiplier claims before presenting them as settled fact.

## Story Economy Lens

- Attention is scarce capital.
- Narrative gives meaning to assets, products, people, and strategy.
- Whoever frames the story often frames the value.
- Use this lens when working on:
  - fundraising or investor narrative
  - founder story
  - product positioning
  - leadership communication
  - personal brand or reputation building

Questions to ask:

- Is this content fighting for attention, trust, action, or valuation?
- What does this story make more valuable?
- What meaning does the current draft fail to assign?

## AI Collaboration Lens

- AI lowers the cost of producing average content.
- That makes `taste`, `selection`, and `lived specificity` more valuable.
- The user summary frames this risk as the `Vortex of Mid`.
- Operational rule:
  1. Think independently first.
  2. Use AI to expand, compress, compare, and iterate.
  3. Keep final taste judgments with the human.

Signs of `mid`:

- polished but bloodless
- coherent but unsurprising
- full of claims, light on stakes
- structurally correct, emotionally empty
- many options, no point of view

## Working Model For This Skill

1. Story is leverage under attention scarcity.
2. AI tends toward averages unless a human supplies taste, stakes, and selection.
3. A useful story shifts a belief, not just formats information.
4. Vulnerability earns trust only when it reveals a real cost, not when it performs authenticity.
5. Good outputs keep the author in charge of the final artistic choice.
6. Good structure starts with a sharp top-line claim before it becomes a scene-by-scene experience.
7. Story quality is partly a selection problem: the winning detail is often more important than more text.

## Architecture Selector

### Use Pyramid Principle when

- the user is writing non-fiction
- the argument is concept-heavy
- the piece needs a clean top-line message
- the audience needs orientation fast

### Use Story Spine when

- the user is leading change
- the audience must understand how one state turned into another
- the piece needs momentum and sequence

### Use Hero's Journey when

- the user is persuading or selling
- a customer, user, or team member should be the hero
- the transformation matters more than the chronology

### Use Scene -> Ouch -> Insight -> Choice when

- the user is writing a personal essay
- the emotional truth is the asset
- vulnerability is central to trust

## Diagnostic Questions

- What is the top-line claim of this piece?
- Which three supporting points hold it up?
- What old belief is the draft still trapped in?
- What new belief does the story want the audience to adopt?
- Where is the real human cost?
- What would make this audience say "that is me"?
- What is the single most surprising truthful observation here?
- What tension gap opens in the first 3 lines?
- What should the audience do, believe, or feel next?

## Medium-Specific Emphasis

### Founder Story

- Show the false start before the insight.
- Name the cost of staying with the old worldview.
- Let the founder sound changed, not polished.

### Sales Story

- Lead with the cost of the status quo.
- Make the buyer's risk visible before the product.
- Turn proof into scenes, not slogans.

### Leadership Memo Or Talk

- Clarify what changed, why now, and what tradeoff follows.
- Use one story to carry the strategic point.
- End with the decision people need to make.

### Essay Or Article

- Start with a scene, contradiction, or sharp observation.
- Delay the thesis just enough to create curiosity.
- Return each section to the same core tension.

## Prompt Seed

Use this when a separate agent or tool needs a compact briefing:

```text
Act as a demanding human narrative coach. First compress the draft into a Pyramid Principle top-line plus three supports. Then find the real human "ouch", diagnose it with RENT, identify the Big Idea, define the single audience muse, choose the best narrative architecture, and rebuild the piece so it escapes the Vortex of Mid. Give options and tradeoffs before a final rewrite. Do not invent facts or steal the author's final artistic choice.
```
